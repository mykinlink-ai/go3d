How a Family Crest Is Traditionally Built

Family crests (coats of arms) come from heraldry, a system developed in medieval Europe to identify families, individuals, and institutions. Each part of the design has a purpose and meaning.

Family / House Name

The family or house name identifies who the crest belongs to. It often appears above or near the crest.

The Crest (Above the Shield)

The crest sits above the shield, often on a helmet or crown. It might be an animal, object, or symbol that represents honor, rank, or identity.

The Shield and Its Quarters

The shield is the heart of the coat of arms. It can be divided into sections (quarters), each representing something important:

  • Top Left (Dexter Chief): Position of greatest honor; main trait, lineage, or defining symbol.
  • Top Right (Sinister Chief): Alliances, partnerships, or secondary strengths.
  • Bottom Left (Dexter Base): Personal virtues, profession, or inherited symbols.
  • Bottom Right (Sinister Base): Aspirations, future goals, or legacy.

Symbols (Charges)

Symbols on the shield are called charges. They are chosen for meaning:

  • Animals: courage, leadership, nobility
  • Plants: growth, peace, renewal
  • Tools: knowledge, justice, craftsmanship
  • Stars / Sun / Moon: guidance, hope, divine favor
  • Mythical creatures: transformation, protection, strength

Supporters, Mantling, and Compartment

Supporters are figures (often animals) standing on each side of the shield. Mantling is the decorative cloth flowing from the helmet. The compartment is the base they stand on (rock, scroll, ground, etc.). These elements add story, status, and style.

Motto

The motto expresses the guiding principle or philosophy of the family. It often appears on a scroll beneath the shield.

Colors (Tinctures)

Traditional heraldic colors (tinctures) include gold, silver, red, blue, green, black, and purple. Each color carries symbolic meaning, such as courage, loyalty, hope, or wisdom.
